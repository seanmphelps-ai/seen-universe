# Location Philosophy — Place, Incubator, Exposure, Forge

## Canonical language

**Place is the incubator. Environmental exposure is what accumulates inside it. The Forge is the shaping process produced by repeated contact over time.**

Location establishes environmental conditions and exposure potential. It does not establish a person's outcome by itself.

## Product model

```text
location conditions × duration × recurrence × intensity × pathway = exposure dose
```

SEEN retains separate historical slices for:

1. birth location;
2. each location lived for six months or longer;
3. current location.

Six months is SEEN's minimum residence threshold. It is a product and modeling rule: enough sustained contact for a location to qualify for evaluation. It is not presented as a universal biological deadline.

Each location may contribute distinct physical, social, economic, cultural, institutional, geographic, and temporal conditions. Duration changes estimated dose. Recurrence, intensity, pathway, evidence quality, and historical fit determine what the system may support.

## Canonical distinctions

- Location supplies exposure potential, not exposure magnitude by itself.
- Exposure magnitude requires conditions plus time and relevant pathways.
- Environmental evidence describes an environment before it describes a person.
- A supported environmental pattern may become a conditioning input. It never proves an individual's belief, adaptation, diagnosis, motive, or destiny.
- Missing historical evidence remains unavailable. Current evidence must not replace it.
- Contradictory conditions remain visible rather than flattened.

## Approved user-facing phrases

- Reveal the places that shaped the pressure.
- Six months is enough time for a place to leave an imprint.
- Behaviors that once required effort may become automatic.
- Same structure. Different incubator. Different expression.
- Not fully at home anymore, but never fully part of the new place.
- The roots may grab hold and change the tree's trajectory.

## Claim boundary

Metaphors such as breaking an old self, forging a new self, roots taking hold, or a seed withering may support product voice. They must not appear as scientific guarantees.

SEEN may say an environment **may shape**, **may reinforce**, **appears associated with**, or **provides conditions for** an adaptation when evidence supports that language. SEEN must not claim a location deterministically rewired a person or caused a personal outcome.
